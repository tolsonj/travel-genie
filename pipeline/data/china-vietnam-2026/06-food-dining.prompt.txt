### CHINA — BEIJING

#### 5 Must-Try Dishes

| Dish | Pronunciation | Order with | Where | Price |
|------|--------------|-----------|-------|-------|
| **Peking Duck** (北京烤鸭) | Běijīng kǎoyā | Pancakes, scallions, hoisin sauce | Da Dong (多地); Quanjude; Duck de Chine | ¥200–400/person (~$28–55) |
| **Zhajiangmian** (炸酱面) | Zhá jiàng miàn | Cucumber shreds, soybean paste | Old Beijing noodle restaurants; Lao Beijing Noodle King | ¥20–40 (~$3–6) |
| **Dumplings (Jiaozi)** (饺子) | Jiǎozi | Vinegar + chili oil dipping sauce | Din Tai Fung (reliable quality); street dumpling shops near hutongs | ¥30–80 (~$4–11) |
| **Hotpot (Huoguo)** (火锅) | Huǒguō | Sesame sauce, lamb, tripe, tofu | Hai Di Lao (24hr, great service); Ghost Street (Guijie) | ¥100–200/person (~$14–28) |
| **Bing Tang Hulu** (冰糖葫芦) | Bīng táng húlu | On its own | Street vendors near Wangfujing; winter snack but sold year-round | ¥5–10 (~$1) |

#### Food Experience
- **Market/hutong food walk**: Self-guided through Nanluoguxiang hutong; try local sesame paste flatbreads (shaobing), stinky tofu, candied hawthorn
- **Peking Duck ceremony**: At Da Dong, the chef carves tableside — the ritual is part of the experience; book Da Dong 2–3 weeks ahead

#### Border Crossing Day (Day 6 — Beijing → Hanoi)
- Airport dining at PEK is adequate; terminal 3 has Cantonese restaurant options
- Pack snacks (hotel concierge can arrange a packed lunch box)
- Arrive Hanoi evening: Pho 24 or street pho near hotel — available until late

#### Food Safety — Beijing
- **Tap water**: DO NOT DRINK — use bottled water always; hotel provides complimentary bottled water
- **Street food**: Generally safe at busy, high-turnover stalls; avoid anything sitting out in heat without cover
- **Ice**: Ask hotels to confirm ice is made with filtered water

#### Dining Customs — China
- Tipping is NOT customary (can actually cause confusion); don't tip
- Meals eaten family-style (shared dishes) — order 1 dish per person + 1 extra
- Slurping noodles is fine; shows appreciation
- Tea is usually free and refilled; ask 再来一壶 (zài lái yī hú = "another pot please")
- Meal timing: Lunch 12–1:30 PM; Dinner 6–8 PM

#### Food Experience — Hanoi
- **Old Quarter food walk**: Evening walk from Hoan Kiem through Ta Hien Beer Street; self-guided
- **Egg coffee ritual**: Sit on tiny plastic stool, watch it being made — thick egg yolk + condensed milk whipped into espresso; a Hanoi original

#### Border Crossing Day — Hanoi → Đà Nẵng (Day 8)
- Morning: Full breakfast at Metropole (exceptional Vietnamese breakfast spread)
- Airport food: Noi Bai has limited options — eat before departing hotel
- Đà Nẵng arrival: Resort restaurant for late lunch; or Grab to a Mỹ Khê Beach seafood spot

#### Food Safety — Vietnam
- **Tap water**: DO NOT DRINK in either city; bottled water universally available
- **Street food safety**: Go where locals go; look for high turnover stalls; avoid places with flies or unrefrigerated meat
- **Ice safety**: Resort/hotel ice is safe; street stalls — ask if filtered (đá lọc)
- **Raw vegetables**: Fine at established restaurants; more caution at market stalls

#### Dining Customs — Vietnam
- Tipping: Not mandatory but appreciated (10–15% at tourist restaurants; round up at street stalls)
- Meals: Served all at once, shared style
- Eating in order: Locals eat very fast; don't wait for everyone before starting
- Timing: Vietnamese eat early — breakfast 6–8 AM, lunch 11:30–1 PM, dinner 5:30–7:30 PM
- "No" to a dish: "Không, cảm ơn" (Khong, cam on = No thank you)
