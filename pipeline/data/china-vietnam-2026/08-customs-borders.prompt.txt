# Customs & Borders

## Reasoning

Route involves 3 international border crossings: US→China (air), China→Vietnam (air), Vietnam→US (air). All crossings are via international airports — no overland crossings. China and Vietnam are not part of any customs union. The US→China crossing is the most complex (customs declaration required; stricter inspection protocols). The most practically relevant "border" for shopping is the final US customs re-entry.

## Output

---

### Border 1: US → China (Beijing PEK/PKX)

**Customs clearing**: At PEK, you clear Chinese customs upon arrival.

| Item | Rule |
|------|------|
| **Cash** | Declare if carrying >¥20,000 CNY or >$5,000 USD equivalent in cash |
| **Electronics** | No declaration required for personal-use laptops, phones, cameras |
| **Medications** | Prescription drugs: carry original prescription + doctor's note (see [[14-health-safety]]); some medications (codeine, strong sleep aids) require special permits |
| **Food** | Fresh fruits, vegetables, and most meat products from US are prohibited entry into China |
| **Tobacco** | 400 cigarettes duty-free |
| **Alcohol** | 1.5 liters duty-free |
| **Goods** | Personal-use goods valued under ¥5,000 (~$700 USD) generally duty-free |

**Protocol**:
- Fill out Chinese customs declaration card on plane or at kiosk in arrivals
- Green channel (nothing to declare) vs Red channel (items to declare)
- Biometric enrollment at first-ever entry to China: fingerprints + photo
- Immigration queue: Foreigners queue; 30–60 min typical at PEK

**Scams**: None at PEK customs specifically; do NOT accept "help" from people outside the formal immigration/customs queue.

---

### Border 2: China → Vietnam (Beijing PEK → Hanoi HAN)

**Customs clearing**: You exit Chinese customs at PEK (departure security/emigration) and enter Vietnamese customs at Noi Bai.

**China exit (PEK departure)**:
- Emigration stamp in passport
- Currency: May carry out up to ¥20,000 CNY or $5,000 USD without declaration
- Purchased goods: VAT refund available at PEK for eligible purchases (see VAT Refund section)
- No Chinese food products across international borders

**Vietnam entry (Noi Bai HAN)**:

| Item | Rule |
|------|------|
| **Duty-free allowances** | 1.5 liters wine + 1.5 liters spirits; 400 cigarettes; personal goods reasonable value |
| **Cash** | Declare if carrying >$5,000 USD or equivalent |
| **Electronics** | Personal use: no declaration |
| **Medications** | Carry prescription + sufficient supply; controlled substances require permits |
| **Restricted** | No pornography, politically sensitive material; no VPN hardware (software is fine) |

**Crossing duration**: Noi Bai immigration is typically fast for tourists with e-visa (10–30 min); e-visa holders have dedicated queue at most times.

### Border 3: Vietnam → US (Đà Nẵng DAD departure → US arrival)

**This is the most relevant border for customs strategy (re-entering US with purchases).**

**US Customs duty-free allowance** (per person):

| Category | Allowance |
|----------|-----------|
| Total goods value | $800/person duty-free |
| Alcohol | 1 liter duty-free |
| Tobacco | 200 cigarettes (1 carton) duty-free |
| Above $800 | 3–5% duty on next $1,000; higher brackets for luxury goods |
| **Per couple** | $1,600 combined duty-free |

**Restricted/prohibited items entering US from Vietnam/China**:
- No fresh fruits, vegetables, or meats (even packaged fresh products)
- No live plants without USDA permit
- No cultural artifacts without export permit from originating country
- No counterfeit goods (these are confiscated and fines can be issued)
- Endangered species products (coral, ivory, certain shells) — prohibited; do not buy

**Items to declare on CBP Form 6059B**:
- All merchandise acquired abroad
- Gifts brought home for others
- Commercial quantities of anything

**VAT Refund availability** (relevant for shopping in Vietnam):
- Vietnam offers VAT refunds for tourists at major airports; rate is 10% on purchases ≥₫2,000,000 (~$80) per item at participating retailers
- **Process**: Get VAT invoice (hóa đơn VAT) from retailer; fill out refund form; submit at airport VAT refund counter at DAD (Đà Nẵng Airport); cash or credit card refund
- **In practice**: Less systematic than EU — higher-end shops and department stores are most reliable; many small shops cannot process; factor into shopping planning

**China VAT refund (at PEK)**:
- China offers VAT refunds (11–13%) for purchases at designated tax-free shops (marked with "Tax Free" sign)
- **Minimum**: ¥500 (~$70) per single receipt from same store
- **Process**: Get refund form from store; stamp from Customs at PEK departure; cash refund at refund counter before security
- **Eligible shops**: Most luxury malls; Wangfujing department stores; international brand stores; NOT markets like Silk Street

**Customs declaration strategy for this trip**:
- With $20,000 budget, you will likely exceed the $800/person ($1,600 combined) duty-free threshold significantly with watches, handbags, tailored clothing, etc.
- **Recommendation**: Register high-value items you're taking FROM the US (watches, jewelry) on CBP Form 4457 at the port of departure — this proves they weren't purchased abroad and won't be double-taxed on return
- Keep ALL receipts for purchases abroad; use them if questioned at US customs
- Luxury goods (watches, handbags): US duty on most luxury goods from China and Vietnam is 3–5% above the first $800; on $5,000 of purchases, rough duty = ~$170–250 total (very manageable vs the savings)

| Country | Best Buy | VAT Refund | Notes |
|---------|---------|-----------|-------|
| **China** | Silk goods, lacquerware, tea, traditional crafts | Yes at designated stores; 11–13% | Collect forms at purchase; redeem at PEK |

- Tailored clothing: Vietnam (Hội An) — best value on earth for custom garments
