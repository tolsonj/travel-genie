# Cultural Etiquette & Language

## Reasoning

**Context:** First-time solo traveler to Japan with limited international experience. Cultural norms differ significantly from US expectations. Etiquette missteps can cause social discomfort or service denial.

**Key considerations:**
- Japan has high-context communication culture (indirect, non-confrontational)
- Public behavior emphasizes group harmony over individual expression
- Service industry operates on respect-based interactions (not tipping)
- Language barrier exists but manageable with preparation

**Approach:** Focus on 10 daily etiquette rules that prevent embarrassment, 10 survival phrases for independence, and 5 conversation starters to break cultural barriers.

## Output

### 1. Daily Etiquette: 10 Behavioral Norms

#### 1. **Shoes Off Indoors (Genkan Protocol)**
**Rule:** Remove shoes when entering homes, traditional restaurants, temples, ryokan, some changing rooms.  
**How to tell:** Look for raised floor (genkan step) or shoe racks at entrance.  
**Action:** Take off shoes, step up to main floor, turn shoes to face exit (optional but polite).  
**Exception:** Modern restaurants, hotels, stores = shoes stay on.

#### 2. **Quiet on Trains**
**Rule:** No phone calls, minimal conversation, headphone volume low.  
**Why:** Public transit = shared space requiring wa (harmony).  
**Action:** Set phone to silent, type texts instead of calling, speak in whisper if necessary.  
**Exception:** Shinkansen has designated phone areas between cars.

#### 3. **No Eating While Walking**
**Rule:** Eat food where you buy it (stand by vendor) or sit down.  
**Why:** Eating while moving = disrespectful to food and messy.  
**Action:** Buy crepe/snack → stop walking → eat → resume walking.  
**Exception:** Festivals (street food culture during matsuri is exempt), ice cream cones (tolerated).

#### 4. **Bow as Greeting (Not Handshake)**
**Rule:** Slight bow (15° angle) when greeting, thanking, apologizing.  
**How:** Hands at sides, bend from waist, hold 1 second, straighten.  
**Depth:** Casual bow (15°), polite bow (30°), formal bow (45°, not needed for tourists).  
**Exception:** Younger Japanese may offer handshake to foreigners; follow their lead.

#### 5. **Chopstick Etiquette**
**Rules to never break:**
- ❌ Never stick chopsticks upright in rice (funeral ritual for the dead)
- ❌ Never pass food chopstick-to-chopstick (funeral rite)
- ❌ Never point/wave chopsticks at people
- ✅ Place chopsticks on holder (hashioki) when not using
- ✅ Use serving chopsticks (toribashi) for shared dishes if provided

#### 6. **Slurping is Polite**
**Rule:** Slurp ramen, soba, udon noodles loudly.  
**Why:** Shows appreciation, cools noodles, releases aroma.  
**Action:** Lift noodles, suck in audibly, don't worry about noise.  
**Exception:** Quiet eating for rice, side dishes, desserts.

#### 7. **No Tipping**
**Rule:** Tipping is insulting (implies service was inadequate requiring extra incentive).  
**Action:** Pay exact bill, say "gochisousama deshita" (thank you for the meal), leave.  
**Exception:** Tour guides may accept tips (discreetly in envelope), but not expected.  
**Taxi rounding:** Don't say "keep the change" → awkward silence + returned change.

#### 8. **Queue Culture (Line Etiquette)**
**Rule:** Form orderly lines (for trains, shops, ATMs), no cutting, no crowding.  
**Train boarding:** Wait for passengers to exit before entering, line up at floor markings.  
**Escalators:** Stand on left (Tokyo) or right (Osaka), walk on opposite side.  
**Exception:** Rush hour trains = crowding inevitable but still orderly.

#### 9. **Inside Voice (Volume Control)**
**Rule:** Speak quietly in public (trains, restaurants, streets, elevators).  
**Why:** Loud conversation = childish or drunk (negative perception).  
**Action:** Lower voice by 30% vs US normal speaking volume.  
**Exception:** Izakaya (pub) after 8pm = louder conversation acceptable.

#### 10. **Trash Management (Carry Your Waste)**
**Rule:** Public trash cans rare outside convenience stores/train stations.  
**Action:** Carry small bag for trash, dispose at hotel or convenience store.  
**Why:** 1995 Sarin gas attack led to trash can removal for security; sorting culture is intense.  
**Sorting:** Burnable, non-burnable, plastic, cans, bottles (follow bin labels).

### 2. Survival Language: 10 Essential Phrases

| Phrase | Romaji | When to Use |
|--------|--------|-------------|
| **Hello / Good afternoon** | Konnichiwa (kon-nee-chee-wah) | Daytime greeting (10am-6pm) |
| **Good morning** | Ohayou gozaimasu (oh-ha-yoh go-zai-mas) | Morning greeting (before 10am) |
| **Thank you** | Arigatou gozaimasu (ah-ree-gah-toh go-zai-mas) | Any gratitude situation (formal) |
| **Excuse me / Sorry** | Sumimasen (soo-mee-mah-sen) | Getting attention, apologizing, asking for help |
| **Yes / No** | Hai (hi) / Iie (ee-eh) | Confirming or declining |
| **I don't understand** | Wakarimasen (wah-kah-ree-mah-sen) | Language barrier situations |
| **English, please?** | Eigo wa dekimasu ka? (ay-go wah deh-kee-mas kah) | Asking if staff speaks English |
| **How much?** | Ikura desu ka? (ee-koo-rah des kah) | Price inquiries |
| **Check, please** | Okaikei onegaishimasu (oh-kai-kay oh-neh-guy-she-mas) | Restaurant bill |
| **Delicious!** | Oishii! (oh-ee-shee) | Complimenting food (makes chefs smile) |

**Bonus phrases:**
- "Kore kudasai" (koh-reh koo-dah-sai) = "This one, please" (point at menu/item)
- "Toire wa doko desu ka?" (toy-reh wah doh-koh des kah) = "Where is the toilet?"

**Pronunciation tip:** Japanese is phonetic. Every vowel pronounced: a (ah), i (ee), u (oo), e (eh), o (oh).

### 3. Conversation Starters: 5 Questions Locals Enjoy

#### 1. **"Osusume wa nan desu ka?" (Oh-soo-soo-meh wah nan des kah?)**
**Translation:** "What do you recommend?"  
**Why it works:** Shows trust in local expertise, flatters their knowledge.  
**Use:** Restaurants, shops, train stations (for sightseeing tips).

#### 2. **"Kono atari de oishii mise wa arimasu ka?" (Koh-noh ah-tah-ree deh oh-ee-shee mee-seh wah ah-ree-mas kah?)**
**Translation:** "Are there delicious restaurants around here?"  
**Why it works:** Japanese love recommending food, sparks enthusiastic responses.  
**Use:** Ask hotel staff, shopkeepers, fellow train passengers.

#### 3. **"Hajimete Nihon ni kimashita!" (Hah-jee-meh-teh nee-hon nee kee-mash-tah!)**
**Translation:** "This is my first time in Japan!"  
**Why it works:** Invokes hospitality instinct, locals will offer extra help/tips.  
**Use:** When struggling with ticket machines, getting lost, or making small talk.

#### 4. **"Kyoto/Tokyo/Osaka wa totemo kirei desu ne!" (Kyo-to wah toh-teh-moh kee-ray des neh!)**
**Translation:** "[City] is so beautiful!"  
**Why it works:** Complimenting their home = instant warmth, pride in local culture.  
**Use:** Taxis, izakaya, casual conversations.

#### 5. **"Shashin wo totte mo ii desu ka?" (Shah-sheen woh tot-teh moh ee des kah?)**
**Translation:** "May I take a photo?"  
**Why it works:** Asking permission = respectful (vs entitled tourist behavior).  
**Use:** Temples, shrines, private gardens, before photographing people.

### 4. Gestures & Taboos

#### ✅ **Polite Gestures**

**Pointing to yourself:** Touch your nose (not your chest).  
**Indicating "no" or "not available":** Cross arms in X shape (wave hands in front of face).  
**Beckoning someone:** Palm down, fingers waving toward you (NOT palm-up "come here" = rude).  
**Giving/receiving business cards:** Use both hands, slight bow, read card before pocketing.  
**Entering someone's home:** Bring small gift (omiyage), hand it with "tsumaranai mono desu ga" (it's nothing special, but...).

#### ❌ **Taboos (What NOT to Do)**

**1. Blowing nose in public:** Use tissue discreetly, step aside, or go to restroom. Loud honking = gross.  
**2. Public displays of affection:** Holding hands OK, kissing/hugging = uncomfortable for locals.  
**3. Pointing with finger:** Use open hand (all fingers together) to indicate direction.  
**4. Tattoo visibility:** Cover tattoos in onsen (hot springs), some pools, traditional inns. Use bandages/rash guards if large tattoos.  
**5. Walking while smoking:** Illegal in many cities (designated smoking areas only).  
**6. Jaywalking:** Wait for green light even if no cars (everyone else waits, join them).  
**7. Littering:** No trash cans = carry your waste (see Etiquette Rule #10).  
**8. Touching products excessively:** Browse respectfully, don't handle items unless buying.  
**9. Being late:** Punctuality sacred (if meeting someone, arrive 5min early).  
**10. Refilling your own drink:** At group dinners, refill others' glasses (they'll refill yours = social reciprocity).

#### ⚠️ **Religious Site Etiquette**

**Shrines (Shinto):**
1. Bow at torii gate entrance
2. Purify at water basin (temizuya): left hand → right hand → rinse mouth (don't drink) → left hand again
3. Approach shrine, toss coin (5¥ good luck), bow twice, clap twice, bow once, pray silently
4. No photos inside worship hall (outer grounds OK)

**Temples (Buddhist):**
1. Remove hat before entering
2. Incense purification (if available): waft smoke over body for cleansing
3. Bow before Buddha statues, quiet prayer
4. No photos of Buddha faces (bodies/statues from behind OK)

**Both:**
- Don't walk through center of path (reserved for gods/spirits)
- Speak quietly, no phones
- Shoes off if entering interior halls

### 5. Supporting Locals: How to Spend Ethically

#### **1. Eat at Family-Run Restaurants**
**How:** Avoid chain restaurants in tourist zones (Ichiran, Yoshinoya OK but not "local")  
**Find:** Walk 2-3 blocks from main streets, look for Japanese-only menus (Google Translate camera helps)  
**Impact:** Money goes to family, not corporation

#### **2. Shop at Local Markets (Not Department Stores)**
**Where:** Miyagawa Market (Takayama), Nishiki Market (Kyoto), Kuromon Market (Osaka)  
**Buy:** Crafts, snacks, local products (not mass-produced souvenirs)  
**Impact:** Supports regional artisans and farmers

#### **3. Stay at Guesthouses/Ryokan (Not Just Hotels)**
**Why:** Traditional inns are family-operated, cultural experience + economic support  
**Book:** Takayama guesthouse (Day 5-7), consider 1 night ryokan in Kyoto/Hakone  
**Impact:** Tourism revenue distributed beyond major chains

#### **4. Use Small Shops Over Konbini (Sometimes)**
**Balance:** Convenience stores are great (and local franchises), but...  
**Alternative:** Buy snacks at local bakery, drinks at neighborhood liquor shop, lunch at market stall  
**Impact:** Diversifies spending, supports small businesses

#### **5. Tip Tour Guides (Discreetly)**
**When:** If joining food tour, private guide, or exceptional service  
**How:** Cash in envelope (or folded bill), hand with both hands, say "osewa ni narimashita" (thank you for your help)  
**Amount:** ¥1,000-3,000 (~$7-20) for half-day guide  
**Impact:** Guides are often freelance, appreciate gesture even if they initially refuse (insist gently once, then respect refusal)

#### **6. Avoid Over-Touristed "Instagram Traps"**
**Problem:** Gion geisha-chasing, Shibuya Crossing crowding, Fushimi Inari littering  
**Solution:** Visit during off-hours (early morning), respect photo boundaries, don't block paths  
**Impact:** Reduces local resentment of tourism, preserves sites for future travelers

#### **7. Learn Local Craft (Experience Economy)**
**Activities:** Pottery class, sake brewing tour, sashiko stitching, tea ceremony  
**Why:** Pays artisans for knowledge transfer (higher value than buying trinket)  
**Book:** Day 7 (Takayama craft workshop) or Day 10 (Kyoto tea ceremony)
