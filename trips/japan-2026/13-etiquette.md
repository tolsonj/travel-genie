# Etiquette

## Reasoning

**Single country:** No cross-cultural whiplash between nations — but Tokyo fast-urban vs Kyoto traditional-polite is a meaningful tone shift. US traveler assumptions (tipping, loud conversation, eating while walking) need resetting.

**Solo traveler:** Japan is ideal — counter dining, no table-sharing pressure, vending machine culture.

## Output

### Daily etiquette — Japan (10 norms)

| # | Norm |
|---|------|
| 1 | **Bow** slightly when greeting — handshake uncommon |
| 2 | **Remove shoes** when entering homes, ryokan, some temples/restaurants with tatami |
| 3 | **No tipping** — leaving money is awkward, not appreciated |
| 4 | **Quiet on trains** — phone calls forbidden; ringers off |
| 5 | **Stand left on escalators in Tokyo** (right in Osaka/Kyoto — follow locals) |
| 6 | **Do not eat while walking** — stand aside at food stalls or eat seated |
| 7 | **Carry trash** — few public bins; pack out until konbini |
| 8 | **Queue orderly** — never cut lines at stations, attractions, restaurants |
| 9 | **Two hands when giving/receiving** business cards, gifts, or change at shops |
| 10 | **Cover tattoos** in onsen if visible — some ban entirely |

### Survival language (10 phrases)

| English | Japanese | Romaji |
|---------|----------|--------|
| Thank you | ありがとうございます | Arigatō gozaimasu |
| Excuse me / sorry | すみません | Sumimasen |
| One person, please | 一人です | Hitori desu |
| The bill, please | お会計お願いします | Okaikei onegaishimasu |
| Where is ~? | 〜はどこですか | ~ wa doko desu ka? |
| Delicious! | おいしい | Oishii |
| I don't understand | わかりません | Wakarimasen |
| Do you speak English? | 英語を話せますか | Eigo wo hanasemasu ka? |
| This one, please | これをください | Kore wo kudasai |
| Good morning | おはようございます | Ohayō gozaimasu |

### Conversation starters locals enjoy

| # | Question (English) | Japanese approach |
|---|-------------------|-------------------|
| 1 | "What's good to eat here?" | Point at menu + "osusume?" |
| 2 | "Is this line for ~?" | Show ticket + "koko desu ka?" |
| 3 | "Beautiful weather today" | "ii tenki desu ne" — safe opener |
| 4 | "Where are you from?" (they may ask you) | "Amerika kara kimashita" |
| 5 | "This temple is amazing" | Quiet appreciation — don't shout |

### Gestures & taboos

| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Point with open hand | Point with single finger |
| Say "itadakimasu" before eating | Stick chopsticks upright in rice |
| Wait to be seated at restaurants | Pour your own drink if someone poured yours (pour for others) |
| Photograph shrines respectfully | Touch Buddha statues or sacred objects |
| Walk Gion quietly at dusk | Chase or photograph geiko/maiko aggressively |

### Supporting locals

| Action | Impact |
|--------|--------|
| Eat at neighborhood izakaya vs chain | Supports family businesses |
| Buy crafts at Nishiki from makers | Direct to artisans |
| Stay in business hotels vs foreign-only Airbnb | Contributes to local tax base |
| Use coin laundry at hotel | Standard — not insulting |

### Tokyo → Kyoto tone shift

| Aspect | Tokyo | Kyoto |
|--------|-------|-------|
| Pace | Faster, louder stations | Slower, more reserved |
| Dress | Casual everywhere | Slightly more conservative near temples |
| Photography | Generally permissive | Ask in Gion; no flash in temples |
| Transit | Stand left escalator | Buses more common — have exact change or IC |
