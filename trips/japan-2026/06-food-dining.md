# Food & Dining

## Reasoning

**Context:** Food is #2 interest (after nature). Solo traveler in Japan = food paradise with solo-friendly dining culture (counter seating, ramen shops, conveyor belt sushi).

**Budget:** $5,000 total, ~$1,400 allocated to food (14 days × $100/day average)  
**Strategy:** Mix of splurge meals ($60-120), mid-range dining ($20-40), and budget-friendly ($5-15) to average $100/day

**Dietary needs:** None stated (assume omnivore, adventurous eater)  
**Meal mapping:** Align with itinerary activities (early breakfast for hiking days, late dinner after cultural sites)

#### **TOKYO: 5 Must-Try Dishes**

##### **1. Tsukiji-Style Sushi (築地寿司)**

**Pronunciation:** Tsoo-kee-jee soo-shee  
**What:** Ultra-fresh sushi (tuna, salmon, uni, tamago) at market stalls or standing counters  
**Where to order:** Tsukiji Outer Market (Day 2 morning)  
**What to order with:** Miso soup (¥100), green tea (free)  
**Best version:** Sushi Dai (¥3,500 omakase = $25), or standing counter sushi (¥1,500 = $10)  
**Price range:** $10-50 depending on venue  

**Pronunciation guide:**
- "Omakase kudasai" (oh-mah-kah-seh koo-dah-sai) = "Chef's choice, please"
- "Oishii!" (oh-ee-shee) = "Delicious!"

**Neighborhood:** Tsukiji (築地), 20min subway from Asakusa

**Already in itinerary:** ✅ Day 2 morning

##### **2. Ramen (ラーメン)**

**Pronunciation:** Rah-men  
**What:** Noodle soup, regional styles vary (Tokyo = shoyu/soy sauce broth, tonkotsu = pork bone, miso = Hokkaido-style)  
**Where to order:** Ichiran (solo-friendly booth seating), local ramen-ya shops  
**What to order with:** Gyoza (pot stickers, ¥300 = $2), extra chashu pork (¥200)  
**Best version:** Afuri (yuzu-shoyu ramen, light citrus broth, ~¥1,200 = $8)  
**Price range:** $8-15  

**How to order:** Vending machine outside (buy ticket) → hand to chef → customize form (noodle firmness, broth richness, garlic level)

**Neighborhoods:** Shibuya, Shinjuku (everywhere)

**Already in itinerary:** ✅ Day 2 dinner (Ichiran Shibuya)

##### **3. Tonkatsu (とんかつ) — Breaded Pork Cutlet**

**Pronunciation:** Tohn-kaht-soo  
**What:** Deep-fried breaded pork loin, served with shredded cabbage (unlimited refills), rice, miso soup  
**Where to order:** Tonkatsu Maisen (Harajuku location, ~¥1,500 = $10)  
**What to order with:** Hire-katsu (pork tenderloin, leaner) OR rosu-katsu (pork loin, fattier)  
**Best version:** Butagumi (high-end, ¥3,000 = $21, reservation needed)  
**Price range:** $10-25  

**Sauce ritual:** Grind sesame seeds in mortar (on table) → pour tonkatsu sauce → dip pork

**Neighborhood:** Harajuku (原宿), 5min walk from Meiji Shrine

**Insert into itinerary:** Day 3 lunch (after Mt Takao hike, near Harajuku stop)

##### **4. Monjayaki (もんじゃ焼き) — Tokyo-Style Savory Pancake**

**Pronunciation:** Mohn-jah-yah-kee  
**What:** Liquid batter cooked on griddle (runnier than okonomiyaki), eat directly from hot plate with small spatulas  
**Where to order:** Tsukishima Monjya Street (月島もんじゃストリート), 70+ monjayaki shops  
**What to order with:** Beer or chuhai (highball, ¥400 = $3)  
**Best version:** Moheji (もへじ, classic spot, ~¥1,200 = $8)  
**Price range:** $8-15  

**DIY experience:** Cook it yourself at table (staff will help first time)

**Neighborhood:** Tsukishima (月島), 20min subway from Tokyo Station

**Insert into itinerary:** Day 2 or Day 4 dinner (optional, if craving interactive dining)

##### **5. Yakitori (焼き鳥) — Grilled Chicken Skewers**

**Pronunciation:** Yah-kee-toh-ree  
**What:** Charcoal-grilled chicken skewers (thigh, heart, liver, skin, cartilage, leeks)  
**Where to order:** Omoide Yokocho (思い出横丁, "Piss Alley"), Shinjuku's izakaya alley  
**What to order with:** Highball (ハイボール, whisky soda, ¥400), edamame (¥300)  
**Best version:** Toriki (とり喜, standing bar, ~¥1,500 for 5 skewers + 2 drinks = $10)  
**Price range:** $10-25  

**Order guide:**
- "Momo" (もも) = thigh (safest bet)
- "Negima" (ねぎま) = chicken + scallion
- "Tsukune" (つくね) = chicken meatball
- "Shiro" (しろ) = intestine (adventurous)

**Neighborhood:** Shinjuku (新宿), Omoide Yokocho alley near west exit

**Already in itinerary:** ✅ Day 3 evening

### 4. Food Safety by Region

#### **Tap Water Safety**

✅ **Safe to drink everywhere in Japan** (Tokyo, Kyoto, Osaka, Takayama)  
- Hotels, restaurants, fountains = potable water
- No need to buy bottled water (save $5/day)

**Exception:** Kamikochi (mountain area) — refill at visitor center (tested water), avoid stream water

#### **Street Food Hygiene**

✅ **Japan street food = world's safest**  
- Vendors follow strict health codes
- No "street food poisoning" risk (unlike SE Asia)

**Eat freely:** Takoyaki, yakitori, taiyaki, crepes, festival foods

#### **Raw Fish (Sushi/Sashimi)**

✅ **Safe year-round** (strict regulations on parasites, freezing protocols)  
- Tsukiji sushi (Day 2) = ultra-fresh, zero risk
- Conveyor belt sushi = safe (despite sitting out, high turnover)

**Avoid:** Fugu (blowfish, poisonous if improperly prepared) — requires licensed chef, not worth risk for first-time travelers

#### **Allergies & Dietary Restrictions**

**Common allergens in Japanese food:**
- ❌ Shellfish (uni, ebi, tako in sushi)
- ❌ Soy (shoyu sauce, miso soup ubiquitous)
- ❌ Wheat (udon, ramen noodles)
- ❌ Sesame (tonkatsu sauce, dressings)

**If allergic:** Print allergy card in Japanese ([JapanAllergyCard.com](http://japanallergycard.com)), show to chef before ordering

**Vegetarian:** Challenging (dashi broth = fish-based, even in veggie dishes). Seek Buddhist temple restaurants (shojin ryori, 精進料理) or Indian restaurants.

### 5. Dining Customs (Table Manners, Tipping, Meal Timing)

#### **Tipping: DO NOT TIP**

❌ **Tipping is insulting in Japan**  
- Servers may chase you down to return "forgotten change"
- Bill = final price (no tip line on receipt)

**Exception:** Tour guides may accept discreet envelope tips, but not expected

#### **Meal Timing**

**Breakfast:** 7-9am (many shops don't open until 10am, plan ahead)  
**Lunch:** 11:30am-2pm (office workers flood restaurants 12-1pm, go early or late)  
**Dinner:** 6-9pm (izakaya stay open until 11pm-midnight)

**Late-night options:** Ramen shops (open until 2am), convenience stores (24h), Yoshinoya beef bowls (24h chains)

#### **Table Manners (Beyond Etiquette Section)**

**Chopstick rules:** (See [[13-etiquette]] for full list)  
- Don't stick upright in rice (funeral rite)
- Don't pass food chopstick-to-chopstick

**Slurping:** ✅ Encouraged for ramen, soba, udon (shows appreciation)

**Drinking:** Never pour your own drink (pour for others, they'll pour for yours = social reciprocity)

**"Itadakimasu" before eating:** (Ee-tah-dah-kee-mahs) = "I humbly receive"  
**"Gochisousama deshita" after eating:** (Goh-chee-soh-sah-mah desh-tah) = "Thank you for the meal"

**Payment:** Pay at register (not at table), say "okaikei onegaishimasu" (oh-kai-kay oh-neh-guy-she-mas) = "check, please"
