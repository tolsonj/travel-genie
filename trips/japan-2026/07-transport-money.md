# Transport & Money Management

## Reasoning

**Context:** Single-country trip (Japan), 14 days, 4 regions, extensive rail travel (Tokyo → Alps → Kyoto → Osaka). Solo traveler needs independent navigation + budget-conscious currency strategy.

**Transport challenges:**
- Long-distance: Shinkansen (bullet train) expensive without pass
- Local transit: Subway/bus systems complex but manageable
- Rural access: Kamikochi requires bus (not covered by JR Pass)

**Money factors:**
- Cash-heavy society (credit cards not universal)
- Dynamic currency conversion traps (at ATMs)
- No tipping culture (simplifies budgeting)

**Budget:** $5,000 total, ~$400-500 for transport

## Output

### 1. Long-Distance Transport (Between Regions)

#### **JR Pass Strategy**

##### **JR Pass Options**

| Pass Type | Duration | Price | Coverage |
|-----------|----------|-------|----------|
| **7-Day Ordinary** | 7 consecutive days | ¥50,000 (~$340) | All JR trains (shinkansen, local, buses) |
| **14-Day Ordinary** | 14 consecutive days | ¥80,000 (~$550) | Same coverage, full trip |
| **Regional Pass** | Varies | ¥15,000-25,000 | Specific regions only |

##### **Cost-Benefit Analysis (This Itinerary)**

**Without JR Pass (individual tickets):**
- Tokyo → Takayama (via Nagano/Matsumoto): ¥12,000 (~$82)
- Takayama → Matsumoto → Kyoto: ¥11,000 (~$75)
- Kyoto → Osaka: ¥3,000 (~$20)
- Tokyo local trains (4 days × ¥800/day): ¥3,200 (~$22)
- **Total: ¥29,200 (~$200)**

**With 7-Day JR Pass:** ¥50,000 (~$340)

**Verdict:** ❌ **JR Pass NOT worth it** (costs $340, saves only $200 if activate Day 1)

**Alternative strategy:**
- Buy individual shinkansen tickets (Tokyo → Alps, Alps → Kyoto)
- Use IC card (Suica/Pasmo) for local Tokyo/Kyoto/Osaka trains
- **Total cost:** ~$200 (vs $340 JR Pass)

**Exception:** IF adding side trips (Tokyo → Nikko, Kyoto → Himeji Castle), JR Pass becomes worthwhile. For this itinerary = skip JR Pass.

### 2. Local Transit (Within Cities)

#### **TOKYO: Subway + JR Local Lines**

**Systems:**
- Tokyo Metro (9 lines, orange logo)
- Toei Subway (4 lines, green logo)
- JR Yamanote Line (loop line, connects major stations)

**Payment:** IC card (Suica or Pasmo)

**IC Card Setup:**
- Buy at airport or any major station (¥2,000 = ¥1,500 balance + ¥500 refundable deposit)
- Tap at entrance gate, tap at exit (fare auto-deducted)
- Refill at any station ticket machine (¥1,000 increments)

**Cost:** ¥170-320 per trip (~$1.20-2.20), depending on distance

**Daily estimate:** 3-4 trips/day × ¥250 average = ¥1,000/day (~$7/day)

**Routes from Asakusa (hotel base):**
- Asakusa → Shibuya: 25min, ¥200
- Asakusa → Shinjuku: 30min, ¥230
- Asakusa → Akihabara: 15min, ¥170
- Asakusa → Roppongi: 30min, ¥240

**Apps:**
- Google Maps (real-time transit directions, includes platform numbers)
- Tokyo Subway Navigation (offline, free)

**Tips:**
- Avoid rush hour (7:30-9am, 5:30-7pm) = packed trains (literally pushed in by station staff)
- Green cars = women-only cars during rush hour (clearly marked)
- Priority seating (silver seats) = reserved for elderly/pregnant/disabled

#### **TAKAYAMA: Walking + Occasional Bus**

**Old town:** 100% walkable (5-15min between all sites)

**Bus needed for:**
- Hida Folk Village: 10min bus from station (¥200 = $1.40)
- Kamikochi bus terminal: 15min walk from hotel OR ¥200 taxi

**Cost:** Minimal (<¥500/day = $3/day)

**No IC card needed:** Cash-only buses (exact change preferred, drivers provide change)

#### **KYOTO: Bus + Subway**

**Bus-centric city:** Temples spread across city, buses reach all sites

**Kyoto City Bus:**
- Flat fare: ¥230 (~$1.60) per ride, any distance
- Day pass: ¥700 (~$5) unlimited rides (worth it if 4+ rides/day)
- IC card accepted (Suica/Pasmo from Tokyo works here)

**Bus routes for itinerary:**
- Kyoto Station → Fushimi Inari: Bus #5 (10min)
- Kyoto Station → Kinkaku-ji: Bus #101/#205 (40min)
- Kyoto Station → Arashiyama: Bus #28 (45min)
- Kyoto Station → Ginkaku-ji: Bus #5 (30min)

**Subway (2 lines, minimal coverage):**
- Karasuma Line, Tozai Line (don't reach most temples)
- Use for Kyoto Station ↔ central city (¥220-290)

**Daily estimate:** 4-5 bus rides = ¥920-1,150 ($6-8/day), or buy day pass ¥700 ($5)

**App:** Google Maps (shows bus numbers, real-time arrivals)

**Tips:**
- Enter bus through rear door, exit through front (pay when exiting)
- Press button when approaching your stop (次は nonstop announcements)
- "Sumimasen" (excuse me) to get through crowded bus to exit door

#### **OSAKA: Subway + JR Loop Line**

**Subway (8 lines, extensive):**
- Midosuji Line (red line, main north-south artery)
- Chuo Line, Tanimachi Line (east-west)

**IC card:** Suica/Pasmo from Tokyo works in Osaka

**Cost:** ¥180-320 per trip (~$1.20-2.20)

**Routes from Namba (hotel base):**
- Namba → Osaka Castle: 15min subway, ¥230
- Namba → Sumiyoshi Taisha: 20min Nankai train, ¥260
- Namba → Kansai Airport: 45min Nankai Airport Express, ¥930 (~$6.50)

**Day pass:** Osaka Amazing Pass (¥2,800 = $19, includes unlimited subway + 40 attractions free) — NOT worth it for this itinerary (most attractions not on list)

**Daily estimate:** 3-4 trips/day × ¥250 = ¥750-1,000 ($5-7/day)

**App:** Google Maps

### 4. Money Management (Currency, ATMs, Cards)

#### **Currency Strategy**

**Japanese Yen (¥, JPY):**
- Current rate (2026 estimate): ¥145-150 per $1 USD
- $1 ≈ ¥145
- ¥1,000 ≈ $7
- ¥10,000 ≈ $69

**Cash vs Card Split:**
- 70% cash (restaurants, temples, small shops, transit top-ups)
- 20% IC card (pre-loaded for trains/buses)
- 10% credit card (hotels, large purchases, tax-free shopping)

**Daily cash needs:**
- Food: $50-100 (mostly cash)
- Transport: $5-10 (IC card)
- Activities: $5-20 (cash for temples, card for museums)
- **Total: $60-130/day**

**Recommended:** Withdraw ¥30,000-50,000 ($200-350) every 5-7 days

#### **ATMs (Where to Withdraw)**

**Best ATMs for foreign cards:**
- ✅ **7-Eleven ATMs** (ubiquitous, English interface, 24h, low fees)
- ✅ **Japan Post Bank ATMs** (post offices, English interface, 0% foreign transaction fee for some US banks)
- ✅ **Citibank ATMs** (rare, but 0% ATM fee)

**Avoid:**
- ❌ Bank of Tokyo-Mitsubishi UFJ ATMs (often reject foreign cards)
- ❌ Convenience store ATMs except 7-Eleven (Lawson/FamilyMart sometimes charge extra)

**ATM fees:**
- Japan side: ¥100-300 (~$1-2) per withdrawal
- US bank side: $0-5 (Charles Schwab, Capital One 360 = $0 foreign fees)
- **Total cost:** $1-7 per withdrawal (withdraw larger sums less frequently = minimize fees)

**Recommended US bank cards:**
- Charles Schwab Debit (reimburses all ATM fees worldwide)
- Capital One 360 ($0 foreign transaction fees)
- Fidelity Cash Management ($0 ATM fees)

**ATM locations in itinerary:**
- Tokyo: 7-Eleven every 2-3 blocks (Asakusa area = 10+ locations)
- Takayama: Post office (1 block from old town), 7-Eleven near station
- Kyoto: 7-Eleven at Kyoto Station, post office in central area
- Osaka: 7-Eleven every block in Namba

**Strategy:**
- Day 1: Withdraw ¥40,000 ($275) at Haneda Airport 7-Eleven
- Day 6: Withdraw ¥30,000 ($200) in Takayama (replenish after Kamikochi expenses)
- Day 10: Withdraw ¥30,000 ($200) in Kyoto (final withdrawal)

#### **Dynamic Currency Conversion (DCC) TRAP**

**What it is:** ATM asks "Withdraw in USD or JPY?"

**ALWAYS choose JPY** (Japanese Yen)
- Choosing USD = ATM converts at terrible rate (3-5% markup)
- Choosing JPY = your US bank converts at fair rate (0-1% markup)

**Example:**
- Withdraw ¥10,000 ($69 at fair rate)
- DCC "convenience": $73 (4% markup = $4 lost)
- **Always decline DCC, select JPY**

#### **Credit Cards**

**Acceptance:**
- ✅ Hotels, department stores, chain restaurants (100% acceptance)
- ⚠️ Mid-size restaurants (50% acceptance)
- ❌ Small restaurants, temples, street vendors (cash only)

**Recommended cards (0% foreign transaction fees):**
- Chase Sapphire Preferred/Reserve
- Capital One Venture
- Discover it Miles (also has 0% fees, but limited acceptance in Japan = bring as backup only)

**Chip + PIN:**
- Japan uses chip + PIN (not chip + signature)
- Ensure your card has PIN set (call bank before departure if unsure)

**Credit card strategy:**
- Use for hotels (already pre-paid via Booking.com, but hold on card)
- Use for large purchases ($100+) = easier to dispute fraud
- Use for tax-free shopping (easier record-keeping)
- DON'T use for daily meals/small purchases (many places cash-only, slows down transactions)

#### **Tipping Culture**

❌ **No tipping in Japan** (see [[13-etiquette]] for details)

**Budget simplification:**
- Restaurant bill = final price (no math needed)
- Taxi fare = exact fare shown (no rounding up)
- Hotel stay = no porter tips, no housekeeping tips

**Exception:** Tour guides may accept discreet envelope tips (~¥1,000-3,000 = $7-20), but not expected

#### **Multi-Currency Tracking App**

**Recommended:** XE Currency app (offline mode after initial load)

**Features:**
- Save favorite currencies (USD, JPY)
- Quick conversion (type ¥5,000 = instant $34 display)
- Historical rate charts (track if yen weakening/strengthening during trip)

**Budget tracking:**
- Use Notes app or Splitwise (even for solo tracking)
- Log daily spend: Breakfast ¥800, Lunch ¥1,500, Dinner ¥3,000, Transport ¥1,000, Activities ¥600
- Weekly tally ensures staying within $5,000 budget
