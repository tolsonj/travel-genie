# Health & Safety

## Pre-Trip Health

**Vaccinations:** None required (see [[03-immigration-entry]])  
**Recommended:** Hepatitis A (optional), routine vaccines up-to-date

**Travel insurance:** World Nomads Explorer (~$150 for 14 days)
- Medical coverage: $100,000
- Emergency evacuation: Included
- Adventure sports: Covered (hiking, cycling)

**Prescriptions:** Bring 14-day supply + doctor's letter (see [[03-immigration-entry]] medication rules)

## Health Per Region

**Hospitals:**
- Tokyo: St. Luke's International Hospital (English staff), +81-3-3541-5151
- Kyoto: Kyoto City Hospital, +81-75-311-5311
- Osaka: Osaka University Hospital, +81-6-6879-5111

**Tap water:** Safe to drink everywhere ✅

**Food safety:** Extremely safe (see [[06-food-dining]])

**Heat illness (August):**
- Hydrate: 2-3L water/day
- Electrolytes: Pocari Sweat (sports drink, konbini)
- Shade breaks: Rest in AC during 12-3pm peak heat

## Emergency Protocols

**Emergency numbers:**
- Police: 110
- Ambulance/Fire: 119
- US Embassy Tokyo: +81-3-3224-5000 (24/7)

**First aid kit:**
- Ibuprofen, band-aids, anti-diarrheal, antihistamine, blister treatment, sunscreen SPF 50+

**Common scenarios:**
- Heatstroke: Move to shade, cold water, call 119 if severe
- Food poisoning: Rare (Japan hygiene excellent); if occurs, hydrate, rest, call 119 if dehydration
- Sprained ankle (hiking): RICE protocol, pharmacy for compression wrap (¥800)

## Safety

**Solo traveler safety:** Japan = extremely safe (see [[05-accommodation]] neighborhood ratings)

**Scams:** Virtually non-existent (overcharging rare)

**Neighborhoods to avoid late night:** Kabukicho (Shinjuku red-light district) = not dangerous but uncomfortable solo. Visit 7-9pm if curious, leave by 10pm.

**Document backup:** Passport scan in cloud + physical copy (see [[09-tech-connectivity]])

**Validation:** All regions rated "very safe" for solo travelers. Standard precautions sufficient (no special safety gear needed).
