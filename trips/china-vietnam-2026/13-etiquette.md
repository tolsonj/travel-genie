# Etiquette

## Reasoning

**Two-country arc:** Hong Kong SAR → Vietnam — etiquette resets at the border (language, tipping, bargaining, personal space).

**September travel:** Heat and humidity in both destinations; indoor AC culture in HK contrasts with open-air street dining in Vietnam.

**Shopping-heavy itinerary:** Market haggling norms differ sharply — HK fixed-price malls vs Vietnam tailor/stall negotiation.

**US couple baseline:** US tipping muscle memory and loud conversational style need conscious adjustment in both places.

## Output

### Hong Kong SAR

#### Daily etiquette (10 norms)

| # | Norm | Context |
|---|------|---------|
| 1 | Queue seriously — never cut | MTR doors, cha chaan teng, bus stops |
| 2 | Have payment ready before the counter | Octopus/tap-to-pay; fumbling blocks the line |
| 3 | Keep voice low on MTR and in lifts | Speakerphone and loud calls are rude |
| 4 | Say 唔該 (m̀h'gōi) when passing or requesting | Soft "excuse me / please" — works everywhere |
| 5 | Service charge often printed on bill | Extra tipping optional; small round-up for taxis |
| 6 | Ask before photographing people in markets | Sham Shui Po, Temple Street — merchants especially |
| 7 | Dress modestly at temples and nunneries | Shoulders covered; follow posted photo rules |
| 8 | Look right before crossing — left-hand traffic | UK-style roads; easy to misjudge as a US driver/pedestrian |
| 9 | Avoid loud mainland/HK politics debates in mixed spaces | Assume diners hold complex local views |
| 10 | Carry a light layer for aggressive indoor AC | Thank staff quietly if they adjust temperature |

#### Conversation starters (5)

1. "Where's the best neighbourhood coffee — not the tourist spots?" — safe food/city chat
2. "Which season do you think Hong Kong is most comfortable?" — weather is universally safe
3. "Where do you usually hike on weekends?" — Lantau, Dragon's Back, MacLehose — locals love trail talk
4. "Sai Kung or Stanley — which do you prefer for a day out?" — friendly weekend-debate opener
5. "How long has this shop been here?" — great in Sham Shui Po, Tai Hang, independent boutiques

#### Gestures & taboos

| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Point with open palm, not a single finger | Photograph police or security operations |
| Keep feet off MTR seats and furniture | Rest shoes on someone's home furniture |
| Finish food at home-style meals as respect | Start loud PRC vs HK political debates with strangers |
| Use right hand when handing items | Touch strangers' children for photos without asking |

#### Supporting locals

| Action | How |
|--------|-----|
| Eat at busy dai pai dong and cha chaan teng | Cash/Octopus to family-run kitchens |
| Buy from independent bookshops and gallery cafés | Central/Wan Chai indie scene over airport chains |
| Shop Sham Shui Po electronics/textiles with fair haggling | Pay agreed price — don't renegotiate after handshake |
| Skip aggressively hawker-priced "luxury" tram/tour bundles | Money to operators, not residents |

### Vietnam

#### Daily etiquette (10 norms)

| # | Norm | Context |
|---|------|---------|
| 1 | Smile first — tone matters more than grammar | Warmth opens doors when language fails |
| 2 | Pin location accurately in Grab | Saves arguments; taxis less reliable than ride-hail |
| 3 | Remove shoes where you see piles at the door | Temples, some homes, yoga studios |
| 4 | Use chú/cô (uncle/auntie) patterns with older vendors | Softer voice shows respect |
| 5 | Bargain at markets and street stalls — not printed-menu restaurants | Tailors and Cho Hom: negotiate upfront, agree in writing |
| 6 | Observe queue wedges before pushing in | Organic lines; don't Anglo-rage loudly |
| 7 | Ask before photographing monks, funerals, rituals | Often a hard no — pause and read the room |
| 8 | Avoid US-war humour as an icebreaker | Even ironic jokes land badly with many locals |
| 9 | Mirror toast pace when drinking with elders | Sip modestly; don't chug bia hơi socially |
| 10 | Accept tea with both hands when offered by a host | Subtle gesture; rare for tourists but appreciated |

#### Survival phrases (10) — Vietnamese

| English | Vietnamese (phonetic) |
|---------|------------------------|
| Hello | Xin chào (sin chow) |
| Thank you | Cảm ơn (gam un) |
| Excuse me / sorry | Xin lỗi (sin loy) |
| I don't speak Vietnamese well | Tôi không nói tiếng Việt giỏi (toy khong noy tee-eng Viet yuh) |
| How much? | Bao nhiêu tiền? (bow nyew tee-en) |
| Check please | Tính tiền giúp tôi (ting tee-en zoop toy) |
| Not spicy / a little spicy | Không cay / ít cay (khong kai / eet kai) |
| Any seats? | Ở đây có chỗ không? (uh day kaw chuh khong) |
| Delicious | Ngon lắm (ngon lum) |
| Goodbye — see you again | Hẹn gặp lại (hen gap lie) |

#### Conversation starters (5)

1. "Where do locals eat phở around here — not the tourist strip?" — always welcome food talk
2. "Which season in Hanoi do you like best?" — safe seasonal chat
3. "Where do you usually get egg coffee?" — invites a local favourite
4. "Where do you go on weekends near West Lake?" — weekend rhythm, not politics
5. "What time should we leave for a day trip to Ninh Bình?" — logistics wisdom locals enjoy sharing

#### Gestures & taboos

| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Pass money hand-to-hand calmly | Throw bills on the counter aggressively |
| Bow head slightly when entering a temple | Point sole of foot toward altar or Buddha statue |
| Nod and smile when you don't understand | Rank Vietnam below Thailand on "value for smiles" — tired trope |
| Decline temple donation pressure politely and walk away | Film strangers' children without caretaker consent |

#### Supporting locals

| Action | How |
|--------|-----|
| Eat district phở and bia hơi corners | Money to neighbourhood families, not lobster English-menu strips |
| Pay micro-roaster cafés and Cho Hom tailors with clear written quotes | Agree price, fabric, and delivery date before deposit |
| Round up Grab rides modestly | Often more welcome than performative 20% US-style tipping |
| Buy crafts at artisan cooperatives over airport souvenir chains | Direct to makers when possible |

### Cross-cultural navigation — Hong Kong → Vietnam

#### Etiquette deltas

| Topic | Hong Kong SAR | Vietnam | Adaptation tip |
|-------|---------------|---------|----------------|
| **Tipping** | Service charge common in sit-down dining; extra tip optional. Taxi round-up OK. | Not expected at local stalls; upscale hotels may add service. Round up Grab. | Reset at immigration: HK "bill is final" → VN "cash + small round-up." Don't tip 20% by reflex. |
| **Dining timing** | Lunch 12:00–14:00 rush; dinner peaks 18:30–21:00. Late brunch fits HK café culture. | Breakfast phở from ~06:00; lunch 11:30–13:30; dinner 18:00–20:00. Street stalls wind down early. | Shift earlier in Vietnam — your HK late-brunch habit becomes a light lunch + early street-food dinner. |
| **Personal space** | Tight MTR but orderly queues; strangers rarely chat unprompted. | Closer physical proximity in markets and motorbike traffic; more spontaneous friendliness. | Expect more shoulder-to-shoulder in VN — don't read closeness as aggression. |
| **Bargaining** | Fixed prices in malls; mild negotiation at markets (electronics, bags). Walk away works. | Expected at markets, tailors, some souvenir stalls. Agree everything in writing for suits. | HK: negotiate once, pay agreed price. VN: negotiate fabric, timeline, and alterations before deposit. |

#### Where US-normal reads offensive

| Behaviour | Hong Kong | Vietnam |
|-----------|-----------|---------|
| Loud geopolitical jokes in public | High social risk in mixed dining spaces | Less charged on HK politics, but war humour still toxic |
| Disrespectful temple behaviour | Modest dress enforced; photo bans common | Shoes off, quiet voice, no feet toward altar |
| Filming strangers without consent | Markets and MTR — social fail | Temples, funerals, street children — often offensive |
| Aggressive haggling after agreement | Loses face; vendor may refuse sale | Tailor disputes — get everything written first |

#### Cultural whiplash — coping protocol

| Shift | What flips | Fast fix |
|-------|------------|----------|
| **Language** | Cantonese/English → Vietnamese only outside tourist core | Label phrase cards HK-side vs VN-side; laugh at yourself, don't fake fluency |
| **Payment** | Octopus/cards dominant → cash + Grab + QR mix | Withdraw VND before leaving airport; keep small bills for stalls |
| **Volume & queue** | HK strict quiet queues → VN organic, chatty street life | 20-minute hotel-room decompression before first VN dinner — reset together |
| **Spice & broth** | HK mild default, SZ-influenced chilli optional → VN herb-forward, chilli on table | Order ít cay (little spicy) on first meal; adjust from there |
| **Shopping ethics** | HK imitation-market haggling is transactional → VN tailor relationships need patience | Separate "deal mode" (HK market) from "relationship mode" (VN fittings) |
