# Contingency Plans

## Reasoning

14-day trip across 2 countries in September (monsoon/typhoon season for Vietnam; stable season for Beijing). Typhoon risk in Đà Nẵng is the single highest-probability disruption scenario. China visa/entry complexity is the second-highest risk. Two travelers mean couple-specific contingencies (split itinerary, one gets sick). Budget of $20K provides flexibility for rebooking emergencies. All contact info provided as of May 2026 — verify before travel.

## Output

---

### Scenario 1: Weather Ruins Outdoor Day (Per Country)

**Beijing — Rain Day**
- Forbidden City inner courts have covered walkways; continues with modification
- Summer Palace: covered Long Corridor handles light rain; abandon in heavy rain
- → Pivot to: Beijing Capital Museum (full day; world-class exhibits on Chinese history; indoor; free entry); or Parkview Green Mall (indoor luxury shopping + food)
- **Phone**: None needed — all pivots are self-directed

**Đà Nẵng/Hội An — Typhoon Watch**
- Monitor: [weather.com](https://weather.com) + NOAA Pacific Typhoon Center
- Level 1 (watch): Continue as planned; cancel outdoor sea activities; keep Ancient Town visit (covered walkways)
- Level 2 (warning): Cancel Cham Islands; reschedule outdoor sites; stay at resort (this is when the spa/pool/restaurant experience pays off)
- Level 3 (direct hit): Resort is your shelter; do not attempt to move cities; most typhoon-grade storms pass within 12–18 hours; resorts are built to code
- **App**: NOAA Typhoon Watch alerts; set weather alert for "Da Nang" on your phone

### Scenario 2: Missed Flight/Train Between Countries

**Beijing → Hanoi (Day 6)**
- **Immediately**: Go to Vietnam Airlines or Air China gate agent; request next available flight (several operate daily)
- **Rebooking**: Vietnam Airlines: 1-800-VIETNAMESE (US) or +84-24-3832-0320; Air China: 1-800-882-8122; or use airline app
- **Delay cost**: Hanoi hotel can hold reservation 1 night; call Metropole directly: +84-24-3826-6919
- **If no available flight same day**: Stay extra night Beijing (Peninsula can extend; or book last-minute via Hotels.com); notify Hanoi hotel
- **Trip insurance**: Activate if delay >4 hrs; document everything (boarding passes, gate notification)

**Hanoi → Đà Nẵng (Day 8)**
- Domestic Vietnam flight — reachable same day; multiple daily flights on VietJet/Vietnam Airlines
- VietJet customer service: 1900 1886 (Vietnam)
- Vietnam Airlines domestic: 1900 1100 (Vietnam)
- Resort in Đà Nẵng: Hold room; late check-in is standard

### Scenario 3: Sick in Vietnam, Need to Cross to Next Segment

**(More relevant if extending; in current itinerary: sick in Hanoi, need to fly to Đà Nẵng)**
- **Mild illness** (food sickness, fever): Rest 1 extra day in Hanoi; rebook Hanoi → Đà Nẵng flight (same-day options available); call resort to push arrival
- **Serious illness** (hospital required):
  - Hanoi: Vinmec International Hospital — +84-24-3974-3556; English-speaking staff; 458 Minh Khai, Hai Ba Trung
  - Hanoi: Family Medical Practice — +84-24-3843-0748; Western-standard clinic
  - Đà Nẵng: Da Nang Family Medical Practice — +84-236-3582-699
  - **Travel insurance**: Call your insurer's 24/7 emergency line FIRST before going to hospital for pre-authorization
  - **Do not fly** with high fever — airline can deny boarding; get doctor's clearance note
  - **Adjust itinerary**: Đà Nẵng has enough resort-based time that 1 lost day is recoverable

### Scenario 4: Visa/Entry Denied at Border

**China (Beijing airport)**
- China visa denial at PEK is rare but possible for US citizens
- **Immediate action**: Do NOT leave immigration; request supervisor; have all documents ready (hotel booking, return flight, visa approval letter)
- If denied: Deportation is to the last country of departure; most travelers are returned to US or last transit point
- **Backup routing**: South Korea (Seoul — Incheon) is visa-free for US citizens; Japan (Tokyo) same; both have direct flights from US; restructure trip to start with Vietnam instead (reverse route)
- **Embassy contacts**: US Embassy Beijing — +86-10-8531-4000 (emergency: 24/7)

**Vietnam (Noi Bai airport)**
- Vietnam e-visa rejection at airport is very rare (have printed e-visa approval)
- **If issue arises**: Visa-on-arrival is available at major Vietnamese airports as fallback (must have pre-arranged through a visa service; research "Vietnam VOA letter" before travel)
- **US Embassy Hanoi**: +84-24-3850-5000; Embassy address: 7 Lang Ha, Ba Dinh

### Scenario 5: Lost Passport in a Foreign Country

**Recovery Protocol (Both Countries)**

**Step 1**: File police report IMMEDIATELY — required for embassy and insurance
- China police: 110
- Vietnam police: 113
- Get written report with official stamp

**Step 2**: Contact US Embassy/Consulate
- **US Embassy Beijing**: 55 An Jia Lou Rd, Chaoyang District; +86-10-8531-4000 (emergency)
- **US Embassy Hanoi**: 7 Lang Ha, Ba Dinh; +84-24-3850-5000 (emergency)
- **US Consulate Ho Chi Minh City**: 4 Le Duan, District 1; +84-28-3520-4200

**Step 3**: Emergency passport — US Embassy can issue an emergency passport (1–2 business days); bring:
- Police report
- Proof of citizenship (digital copy of lost passport — stored in iCloud/Google Drive)
- 2 passport photos
- $170 fee

**Step 4**: Notify travel insurance; document everything; hold on flights until new passport confirmed.

### Scenario 6: Political Unrest or Travel Ban

- US State Department emergency line: 1-888-407-4747 (from US) or +1-202-501-4444 (overseas)
- China: Political unrest in Beijing is very low probability; if it occurs, shelter at the hotel; contact embassy; do not attend any gatherings
- Vietnam: Very stable politically; essentially zero risk of sudden disruption for tourists
- **Monitoring**: Register trip at [travel.state.gov/STEP](https://travel.state.gov/STEP) (Smart Traveler Enrollment Program) — US Embassy will contact you directly in an emergency

### Scenario 7: One Partner Wants to Split from Itinerary

- Profile states "vote" as the decision method
- **Procedure**: One partner stays at resort / does their own day; Grab app makes independent navigation easy in Vietnam; DiDi in China
- **Safety protocol**: Share live location via iPhone FindMy or Google Maps location sharing; agree on check-in time
- **In Hội An specifically**: Ancient Town is pedestrian-safe and compact; solo wandering is easy
- **In Beijing**: Language barrier means solo exploration requires DiDi and offline translation app; manageable but more complex than Vietnam

---

### Scenario 8: Budget Overrun in China

*China is the value country; Vietnam is the splurge. If over budget in China:*
- Cut: Silk Market purchases (browse don't buy; better quality in Vietnam anyway)
- Cut: Premium Peking Duck restaurant (Da Dong) → cook at the market option (~¥150 vs ¥400/person)
- Cut: Private Great Wall car → shared shuttle (~¥80 vs ¥400 round trip)
- Cut: One hotel night dinner → street food equivalent ($5 vs $80)
- **In Vietnam**: Already planned as splurge — don't cut resort; can reduce Hội An shopping budget

---

### Scenario 9: Luggage Lost on International Connection

**Home → Beijing**
- File property irregularity report at PEK baggage claim immediately
- Keep carry-on with: 2 days of clothing, all documents, medications, electronics, valuables
- **Airline baggage delay claim**: Most airlines deliver within 24–48 hrs in Beijing; 5-star hotel concierge can coordinate delivery
- **Emergency shopping**: Wangfujing department store has everything; or hotel boutique for immediate needs
- **Travel insurance**: Most policies cover $200–500 for essential purchases during baggage delay >6 hrs; keep receipts

**Between Countries (Beijing → Hanoi)**
- Same protocol at Noi Bai Airport
- **Vietnam Airlines baggage**: File at Noi Bai arrivals; local number +84-24-3832-0320
- Emergency shopping: Hanoi Old Quarter markets are extremely well-stocked for clothing

---

### Scenario 10: Natural Disaster or Border Closure

**Typhoon closes Đà Nẵng Airport (DAD)**
- Monitor: National Hydro-Meteorological Service of Vietnam (nchmf.gov.vn)
- **If airport closes**: Buses operate to Hue or Đà Nẵng train station; train north to Hanoi (14 hrs; seat on train available within hours if booking on 12go.asia)
- **Flights**: Rebook via app when airport reopens; typhoons typically close airports 12–48 hrs
- **Travel insurance**: "Trip interruption" clause covers typhoon-related rebooking costs; call insurer immediately

**Earthquake (low risk)**
- Vietnam and southern China are in lower seismic zones; risk is minimal
