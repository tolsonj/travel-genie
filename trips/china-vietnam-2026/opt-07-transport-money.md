# Transport & Money

---

### LEG 1: Home → Hong Kong

- **Mode**: International flight (Cathay Pacific, United, Singapore Airlines)
- **Airport**: **HKG** (Hong Kong International)
- **Time**: ~15–18 hrs from US West; ~16–20 hrs from US East (1 stop typical)
- **Cost estimate**: $900–1,800/person economy; $3,000+ business
- **Open-jaw**: Return from **DAD** (Đà Nẵng) — book as single itinerary

**Airport → Hotel**
- **Airport Express** to Central/Kowloon (~24 min); Octopus card at station
- **Uber HK / hotel car** — reliable; avoid unmarked curbside taxis per profile
- **Avoid**: Unlicensed “taxi” touts in arrivals hall

- **Mode**: Domestic flight (strongly preferred over 14-hr train for this trip)

### MONEY MANAGEMENT

#### Currencies

| Country | Currency | Code | Approx Rate (2026) |
|---------|----------|------|-------------------|
| China | Chinese Yuan Renminbi | CNY (¥) | ~7.1 CNY = $1 USD |
| Vietnam | Vietnamese Dong | VND (₫) | ~25,000 VND = $1 USD |

#### Exchange Strategy

**China (CNY)**
- **Best**: ATM withdrawal at Bank of China or ICBC ATMs in Beijing; ~2–4% fee vs cash exchange
- **Alternative**: Exchange US dollars at authorized bank counters at PEK (reasonable rate; avoid exchange booths outside banks)
- **WeChat Pay**: Link US Visa/Mastercard directly in WeChat; works at most Chinese vendors including smaller shops (foreigners can now link foreign cards — activate before travel)
- **Cash recommendation**: Keep ¥1,000–1,500 (~$140–210) for incidentals; most 5-star hotels and tourist areas accept cards
- **Avoid**: Dynamic currency conversion (DCC) — always choose to pay in CNY, not USD, at any card terminal

**Vietnam (VND)**
- **Best**: ATM withdrawal at Vietcombank or Agribank ATMs; most charge ₫50,000–100,000 (~$2–4) fixed fee; Charles Schwab card reimburses all fees
- **Cash requirement**: More cash-heavy than China for street food, markets, tailor payments, bicycle rentals
- **Cash recommendation**: Keep ₫3,000,000–5,000,000 (~$120–200) on hand at all times
- **Hotels and restaurants**: All 4–5 star properties accept Visa/Mastercard

#### Multi-Currency Cards (Recommended)

| Card | Best For | Why |
|------|---------|-----|
| **Charles Schwab Debit** | ATM withdrawals | Zero foreign transaction fees; reimburses ALL ATM fees globally |
| **Wise Card** | Everyday purchases | Multi-currency; competitive exchange rate; load CNY and VND in advance |
| **Chase Sapphire Preferred / Reserve** | Hotels, flights, restaurants | 3x travel points; no foreign transaction fee; trip delay insurance built in |
| **Backup Visa/MC** | Emergency | Notify of China AND Vietnam travel; freeze one card at home as backup |

#### ATM Strategy
- Withdraw larger amounts less frequently (minimize fixed ATM fees)
- Beijing: Withdraw ¥2,000–3,000 at airport; top up at Bank of China ATMs in city
- Vietnam: Withdraw ₫2,000,000–3,000,000 at Noi Bai; top up at Vietcombank ATMs
- Always use ATMs at bank branches or inside 4–5 star hotels — not standalone street ATMs

#### Tipping Summary (Cross-reference [[13-etiquette]])

| Service | China | Vietnam |
|---------|-------|---------|
| Restaurant (table service) | No tip | 10–15% or round up |
| Street food | No tip | Round up is kind |
| Hotel housekeeping | ¥20–50/day (optional) | ₫50,000–100,000/day |

#### Budget Tracking
- **Recommended app**: Trabee Pocket (multi-currency budget tracker for travel) or Trail Wallet
- Set daily budget in each currency; log expenses in the evening
- **China daily budget for 2**: ¥2,000–3,000 ($280–420) covers hotel, 2 meals out, transport, incidentals (accommodation separate)
- **Vietnam daily budget for 2**: ₫2,000,000–4,000,000 ($80–160) for food/transport/activities (resort accommodation separate)

### Duty-Free & Customs Overview

*(Full detail in [[08-customs-borders]])*

- **From US to China**: US allowance for carry-on duty-free goods entering China is $5,000 value; declare anything above
- **From China to Vietnam**: Internal airport transit; keep receipts for expensive items
- **Returning to US**: $800/person duty-free exemption; above $800 = 3–5% duty on most goods; expensive watches/jewelry should be registered with US Customs before departure (CBP Form 4457)
